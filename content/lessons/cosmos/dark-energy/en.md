## Intuition

In 1998, two teams of astronomers measured about 50 distant exploding stars called type Ia supernovae, whose true brightness is known, and found them roughly 10–15% dimmer than a slowing-down universe predicted. Dimmer meant farther away than expected, so the expansion had to be speeding up, not braking, the way everyone had assumed.

## Formal

Einstein's equations tie the universe's expansion rate to what fills it. For matter alone, $(\dot a/a)^2 = (8\pi G/3)\rho_m$, where $\rho_m$ is the average matter density and $G$ is Newton's constant. Adding a constant energy density that never thins out as space stretches, written $\Lambda$, changes this to $(\dot a/a)^2 = (8\pi G/3)\rho_m + \Lambda c^2/3$.

Unlike matter, $\Lambda$ does not dilute as the universe grows, so its share of the total energy, called $\Omega_\Lambda$, has been catching up for billions of years and today stands at about 0.68. Once that term dominates, the expansion rate stops shrinking and starts climbing instead — acceleration.

## Advanced

Dark energy is usually modelled as a cosmological constant, the simplest option, but its measured value is bizarrely small compared with what quantum field theory naively predicts — one of the biggest open problems in physics. Whether it truly stays constant or slowly changes with time is being tested by ongoing surveys, and honestly, nobody yet knows what dark energy actually is.

## Derivation

1. Einstein's equations connect how fast the universe expands to the density of matter it contains.
2. Adding a constant term that does not dilute as space stretches lets that term eventually take over the equation.
3. Because that term's share of the total keeps growing, today it makes up roughly two-thirds of everything in the universe.

## Real world

### The 1998 supernova surveys
The High-Z Supernova Search Team and the Supernova Cosmology Project independently found the universe's expansion is accelerating, work that won the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics.

### Measuring ΩΛ from the cosmic microwave background
Tiny patterns in the CMB, combined with supernova data, pin down dark energy's share of the universe to around 68%.

### The universe's ultimate fate
If dark energy stays constant, galaxies outside our local group will eventually recede faster than light can reach us, leaving an ever emptier sky.

### A name for our ignorance
"Dark energy" is really a placeholder name for whatever is causing the acceleration — a cosmological constant, a changing field, or something not yet imagined.
