## Intuition

Drag the price line below where the two curves cross: buyers now want more
than sellers are willing to provide at that low price, so the gap between
the demand and supply lines is a shortage. This is a price ceiling — rent
control is the classic example, holding rent below what the market would
set.

Now drag the price line above the crossing point: sellers want to offer
more than buyers will take off their hands at that high price, and the
gap becomes a surplus. This is a price floor — a minimum wage above the
market wage works the same way in the labour market, with the "sellers"
being workers.

## Formal

At a fixed price $P_0$, the quantity demanded is read off the demand line
and the quantity supplied off the supply line; they generally differ once
$P_0$ is not the equilibrium price. If $P_0$ is below equilibrium, quantity
demanded exceeds quantity supplied: a shortage. If $P_0$ is above
equilibrium, quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded: a surplus.

The size of the gap depends on both how far $P_0$ is from equilibrium and
on how steeply the curves slope — the same price gap produces a bigger
quantity gap when demand or supply is more sensitive to price.

## Advanced

Controls do not remove scarcity, they only change who bears it: a shortage
is rationed by queues, waiting lists or favouritism instead of price, and
a surplus sits as unsold stock or unemployment. Economists broadly agree
that price controls create deadweight loss, though the size of that loss
and whether other goals (affordability, income support) justify it remain
debated, especially for minimum wages where the empirical effect on
employment is smaller than simple theory predicts.

## Derivation

1. Read the quantity demanded off the demand line at the fixed price.
2. Read the quantity supplied off the supply line at the fixed price.
3. Subtract: a positive gap is a shortage, a negative gap is a surplus.

## Real world

### Rent control
Cities that cap rents below market level often see long waiting lists and
landlords reluctant to maintain or offer units.

### Minimum wage
A wage floor above the market rate raises pay for those still employed but
is debated for its effect on low-skill hiring.

### Fuel price caps
Governments capping fuel prices during shortages often see queues at
petrol stations instead of prices rising.

### Wartime rationing
Price ceilings on staple foods during shortages led to ration cards
allocating the scarce supply directly.
